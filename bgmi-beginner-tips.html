BGMI Beginner Tips: Survive the First 10 Matches

By GameTipsHub Team | Updated October 2026

Battlegrounds Mobile India (BGMI) can feel overwhelming when you first drop into a match. Everyone is running, shooting and reading the map at the same time. The good news is that most early deaths come from a handful of avoidable mistakes, not from bad aim. This guide covers the habits that will keep you alive longer and make your first ten matches a lot less stressful.

Pick a landing spot that matches your skill

Hot drops are exciting, but for a beginner they usually end in a fast death. Pick a quieter spot with a few houses and some loot, then move toward the circle when you have a decent kit. Avoid major cities until you are comfortable with the fight, because too many players land there and the first few seconds are chaos.

Loot in the right order

Look for a helmet, armor, a backpack and a weapon first. Health items and grenades come next. Do not spend a minute opening every drawer in a house. Check the big houses quickly, grab what you need and move on. The zone is always closing, and running out of time in the circle is a common reason beginners get caught in the open.

Learn the zone early

The blue zone shrinks in stages. Before each stage starts, look at the map and pick a direction that keeps you near cover. If you are far outside the circle, start moving early instead of waiting for the damage warning. Running through open fields at the last second is the number one way to get finished by a player with a scope.

Use cover, not just speed

A common mistake is running in a straight line while being shot. Move in short bursts, use walls and bushes, and crouch when you stop. When you hear shots but cannot see the enemy, stay behind a building until you have information. Patience wins more early matches than reckless running.

Pick one gun and master it

Beginners often switch weapons every few minutes and never get used to recoil. Choose one assault rifle that fits your playstyle and practice it in the training mode or in a few casual matches. Learn the first few bullets, the recoil pattern and how it feels at medium range. Once that feels natural, you can add a second weapon for close fights.

Squad communication matters

If you play with friends, call out enemy positions, zone updates and the number of players you can see. Short, clear callouts beat long messages. If you are solo, play slower and avoid fights with two or more enemies unless you have a clear advantage.

Practice your sensitivity and HUD layout

Your sensitivity settings affect how fast you can aim and turn. Find a setting where small movements feel controllable. Also set up your buttons so your thumbs do not have to stretch. A comfortable layout makes a big difference in fights.

Your first ten match checklist

Play at least ten matches with a simple goal in each: land in a calm area, loot fast, stay near the zone edge, avoid open fields, and use cover. Track how many times you die in the first five minutes. That number usually drops quickly once you slow down. Survival is more important than kills when you are new. Every match you survive teaches you the map, the zone timing and how players move. Keep your expectations realistic and enjoy the learning process.
